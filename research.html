Working papers

  • Who Talks? Disclosure and Price Inference in Production Networks
    with V. Cuciniello. September 2026

    Supplier information about common cost pressures can reach buyers in two ways—direct disclosure or inference from prices—with different implications for markups, trade, and welfare. We combine a persistent randomized information treatment that shifts firms’ inflation beliefs with administrative records covering the universe of Italian business-to-business transactions. Firms learn from trading partners primarily through communicated beliefs; price-based learning appears only where supplier prices aggregate broad upstream information. Belief transmission concentrates in established supplier relationships and loads on supplier-side rather than buyer-side characteristics, consistent with disclosure sustained by repeated interaction; links with better-informed suppliers also carry higher trade volumes. A model of strategic supplier disclosure rationalizes these patterns: disclosure removes the markup distortion that arises when buyers infer costs from prices and is sustained by relationship surplus. In the calibration, endogenous disclosure eliminates more than 80 percent of the markup-dispersion loss that would arise under price inference alone.
